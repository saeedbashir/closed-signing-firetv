## Found

- `difflib.SequenceMatcher` is not an LCS. It takes the longest matching block
  greedily, and a chamber repeats itself: the First Minister was interrupted and began
  his sentence again, the recogniser misheard one word in each copy, and the second
  copy of "of a Government that inflicted austerity on this country" was pinned to
  the first utterance. The optimum prefers matching both copies. rapidfuzz's
  `LCSseq.editops` does 4,854 × 4,981 in under 10 ms.
- The remaining faults were all found by reading every cue, not by tests, and each
  became a test: navigation links read as speech; lines split at the middle space;
  edited-out speech smeared across silence; a stage direction at the start of its gap
  instead of the middle; the recogniser handing a 4 s pause to the word before it; a
  new speaker's rewritten opening clinging to the previous speaker; one-word orphan
  cues; a colon printed at the start of a speech paragraph.
- Known limit, documented and left: the LCS is not time-aware. "the inquiry?" matched
  the same two words six seconds later in the answer because the member said "that
  inquiry?" — two cues, once, in 34 minutes.
- Process: `pytest | tail -1` reports `tail`'s exit code. A red test started a
  21-minute pipeline run before I saw it. The gate is a script now (`gate.sh`, exits
  non-zero). And a running `stm run` has already imported the aligner — edits during
  the encodes do not reach it; three restarts today taught that.
